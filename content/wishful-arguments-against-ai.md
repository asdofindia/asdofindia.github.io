##### Some arguments against AI are really important to consider. Others are simply non-sequitors that pile on #####

### AI is not intelligent

AI is intelligent enough to be called intelligent. It gives answers and solves problems, on average better than an idiotic human being. (And even an idiotic human being will be considered to be possessing "intelligence", although the measure of that in arbitrary tests like "IQ" will be less.)

Being right all the time is not called "intelligence" it is called "omniscience". It is not AO, it is AI.

So, AI can be wrong, and that doesn't disqualify it from being called AI.


### AI destroys independent web

Without data, this doesn't mean much. AI helps people set up websites independently. So, AI creates independent web too.

I have been running several websites (including this blog) on the smallest VPS of hetzner. Even before AI, and even after AI. The server runs a mediawiki instance, 8 wordpress instances, 15+ static sites, and a couple of other database intensive services. 

In the beginning, with content scraping, the php-fpm used to get choked. But with some tweaks in configuration (increasing workers, setting up cache on wordpress and preventing access to history/diff for non-logged-in users on mediawiki) and switching some sites to frankenphp, I've not had any issue. I never had to use cloudflare or anubis or captcha or anything to keep the sites live.

### Violation of copyright

I do not believe that copyright is an inviolable principle. There is indeed inequity in who is able to violate copyright without consequences. It is completely unfair that models are being kept as "proprietary" and it would be completely fair to free such models from the gatekeepers and release them for anyone to use.

### Destroying rare physical books

It is wrong to destroy books, especially if they're rare.

### Exploitation of workers

It is wrong to exploit humans. And there's all kinds of exploitation going on in all kinds of labor sectors.

### Exploitation of nature

It is wrong to exploit nature for greed. And there's all kinds of such exploitation going on.

### Privacy

The choice of how much privacy is needed should always be left to individuals.

### Deskilling

Deskilling happens because of misuse. It is possible to increase skills using AI, if used to that end. And vice versa.

### Burnout of free software maintainers

Only popular projects get overwhelmed by AI. There is a long tail of free software projects where AI is _decreasing_ developer effort and prevents burnout.

### Layoffs

AI indeed creates existential questions to humankind. What's the purpose of labor if everything gets automated? Capitalism needs to be uprooted. What to do about bullshit jobs, though? Humans shouldn't have to do meaningless jobs.

### Weakening democracies

Human beings being gullible and un-intelligent is the original weakness of democracies.

### and a lot of other things...

It's all true.

But it's all true about the rest of the world also.

We live in a messy, problematic, risky, dirty world.

### Do I use AI?

I don't use AI when writing things. I don't read AI written articles (and I can say in a few sentences).

When coding, I use AI in some situations (tedious migrations, prototyping frontends, bugfixes).

When using software written by others, I judge based on whether it solves my problems or not. If the use is going to long-term, I judge whether a software project is going to survive long term based on various parameters (including whether the author can code without AI).

I use AI answers (like Gemini) in a wide variety of situations: brainstorming best practices; discovering books, websites, articles, guidelines; quickly quenching curiosities; finding way forward when I'm stuck; etc. I use [&udm=14](https://udm14.com/) to avoid google showing AI answers without me asking for it.
